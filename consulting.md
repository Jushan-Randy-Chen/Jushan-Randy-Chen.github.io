<section id="what" aria-labelledby="what-heading">
  <h2 id="what-heading">What a knowledge graph does for you</h2>
  <p>Most plants already have the answers they need. They're just spread across spreadsheets, ERP and maintenance systems, binders, and the memory of a few experienced people. A knowledge graph connects machines, parts, suppliers, orders, documents and people into one map of how your operation fits together. An AI assistant on top answers questions in plain English and shows where each answer came from, so your team can check it.</p>
  <p>It's also the foundation for agentic AI: assistants that don't just answer, but look things up across your systems, draft work orders and flag supplier or quality risks, with a person approving every action.</p>

<section id="uses" aria-labelledby="uses-heading">
  <h2 id="uses-heading">Where it pays off</h2>
  <ul class="uses">
    <li>
      <h3>Faster troubleshooting</h3>
      <p>Technicians ask "Has this alarm happened before, and what fixed it?" and get answers drawn from past work orders and manuals.</p>
    </li>
    <li>
      <h3>Quality root-cause tracing</h3>
      <p>Follow a defect back through lots, suppliers, machines and process changes without digging through spreadsheets.</p>
    </li>
    <li>
      <h3>Keep tribal knowledge</h3>
      <p>Capture what experienced staff know before they retire, and make SOPs searchable for new hires.</p>
    </li>
    <li>
      <h3>Supplier and part impact</h3>
      <p>See which products, orders and customers are affected when a part or supplier changes.</p>
    </li>
  </ul>
</section>
